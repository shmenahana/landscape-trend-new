## Soil Characteristics (Critical for Landscaping)

### Clay-Heavy Soil
**Why This Matters:**
- Poor drainage without amendments
- Compacts easily (aeration essential)
- Expands/contracts with moisture (retaining wall challenges)
- Needs fall aeration, not spring
- Requires proper backfill for hardscaping

**Typical Challenges:**
- Standing water after rain
- Soil compaction from foot traffic
- Root growth restriction
- Foundation movement if not managed
- Retaining wall failure without drainage

**Solutions:**
- Core aeration annually in fall
- Gravel backfill behind walls
- Drain tile installation
- Amendments for planting beds
- Proper grading

### PH Levels
Typically neutral to slightly acidic (6.0-7.0), good for most Ohio-adapted plants

## Climate & Weather Patterns

**USDA Hardiness Zone:** 6a
**Average First Frost:** Mid to late October
**Average Last Frost:** Mid to late April
**Growing Season:** ~170 days

### Seasonal Characteristics

**Spring (March-May):**
- Unpredictable temps (60° one day, 30° the next)
- Heavy rains common (drainage issues)
- Soil slow to warm due to clay
- Late frosts can damage early plantings
- Prime time for landscape projects

**Summer (June-August):**
- Hot and humid (80-90°F typical)
- Afternoon thunderstorms
- Occasional drought periods
- Not ideal for new plantings
- Grass growth slows in heat

**Fall (September-November):**
- **Perfect weather for landscaping**
- Cooler temps, consistent rain
- Best time for aeration/overseeding
- Ideal for plant establishment
- Oak trees drop late (cleanup extends to Thanksgiving)

**Winter (December-March):**
- Cold (average 20-35°F)
- Variable snow (15-45 inches per year)
- Freeze/thaw cycles (hard on hardscaping)
- Ground frozen December-February
- Ice storms occasional
